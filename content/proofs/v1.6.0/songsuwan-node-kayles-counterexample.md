## What do two more branches change?

In Node-Kayles, choosing a vertex erases it and all its neighbors. The moves left to the next player determine the position’s game value. Songsuwan predicted that if you keep attaching copies of the same rooted tree to one vertex of a fixed graph, adding two more copies eventually leaves that value unchanged.

Take a particular graph on 291 vertices and use five-vertex paths as the branches. With five branches the value is 8. With seven it is 9. The split persists: for every integer $j\geq 0$, the values at $4j+5$ and $4j+7$ branches are 8 and 9. Choose the cutoff as far out as you like and a pair still disagrees. That disproves Conjecture 2. The host has cycles, so the separate conjecture about level-regular trees remains open.
