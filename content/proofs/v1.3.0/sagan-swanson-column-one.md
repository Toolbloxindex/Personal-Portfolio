## What happens between neighboring coefficients?

Look at the coefficients of a polynomial, skipping every other one. Pick three consecutive entries in this shorter list. Sagan and Swanson ask whether the square of the middle entry is always at least the product of its neighbors, a property called parity log-concavity.

For the first-kind type B q-Stirling numbers, the proof establishes the inequality on the rising side of column one at every rank. It also covers the second diagonal in both families. The argument starts from the recurrences defining the polynomials, so one proof applies across ranks. The other columns and diagonals are part of the open question.
